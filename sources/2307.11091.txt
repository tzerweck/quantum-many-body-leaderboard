                                    Data-driven criteria for quantum correlations

                   We build a machine learning model to detect correlations in a three-qubit system using a neural
                network trained in an unsupervised manner on randomly generated states.  The network is forced to
                recognize separable states, and correlated states are detected as anomalies.  Quite surprisingly, we
                find that the proposed detector performs much better at distinguishing a weaker form of quantum
                correlations, namely, the quantum discord, than entanglement.  In fact, it has a tendency to grossly
                overestimate the set of entangled states even at the optimal threshold for entanglement detection,
                while it underestimates the set of discordant states to a much lesser extent.  In order to illustrate the
                nature of states classified as quantum-correlated, we construct a diagram containing various types
                of states – entangled, as well as separable, both discordant and non-discordant.  We find that the
                near-zero value of the recognition loss reproduces the shape of the non-discordant separable states
                with  high  accuracy,  especially  considering  the  non-trivial  shape  of  this  set  on  the  diagram.   The
                network architecture is designed carefully:  it preserves separability,  and its output is equivariant
                with respect to qubit permutations.  We show that the choice of architecture is important to get the
                highest detection accuracy, much better than for a baseline model that just utilizes a partial trace
                operation.
