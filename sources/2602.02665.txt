                  Luciano  Loris  Viteritti,1,       ∗ Riccardo  Rende,2,∗         Subir  Sachdev,2, 3  and  Giuseppe  Carleo1

                   Accessing  the  thermodynamic-limit  properties  of  strongly  correlated  quantum  matter  requires
                simulations  on  very  large  lattices,  a  regime  that  remains  challenging  for  numerical  methods,  es-
                pecially  in  frustrated  two-dimensional  systems.  We  introduce  the  Spatial  Attention  mechanism,  a
                minimal  and  physically  interpretable  inductive  bias  for  Neural-Network  Quantum  States,  imple-
                mented  as  a  single  learned  length  scale  within  the  Transformer  architecture.   This  bias  stabilizes
                large-scale optimization and enables access to thermodynamic-limit physics through highly accurate
                simulations on unprecedented system sizes within the Variational Monte Carlo framework.  Applied
                to the spin- 12  triangular-lattice Heisenberg antiferromagnet,  our approach achieves state-of-the-art
                results  on  clusters  of  up  to  42 × 42  sites.   The  ability  to  simulate  such  large  systems  allows  con-
                trolled ﬁnite-size scaling of energies and order parameters, enabling the extraction of experimentally
                relevant quantities such as spin-wave velocities and uniform susceptibilities.  In turn, we ﬁnd extrap-
                olated thermodynamic limit energies systematically better than those obtained with tensor-network
                approaches  such  as  iPEPS.  The  resulting  magnetization  is  strongly  renormalized,  M0  =  0.148(1)
                (about 30% of the classical value), revealing that less accurate variational states systematically over-
                estimate magnetic order.  Analysis of the optimized wave function further suggests an intrinsically
                non-local sign structure, indicating that the sign problem cannot be removed by local basis transfor-
                mations.  We ﬁnally demonstrate the generality of the method by obtaining state-of-the-art energies
                for  a  J1-J2  Heisenberg  model  on  a  20 × 20  square  lattice,  outperforming  Residual  Convolutional
                Neural Networks.

        I.     FINITE-SIZE  CORRECTIONS  IN  A  QUANTUM  ANTIFERROMAGNET

                                                       A.     Notation

   Given a two-dimensional lattice of L × L spins on a torus generated by primitive lattice vectors
a1  and  a2,  we  view  the  torus  as  the  unit  cell  of  an  inﬁnite  lattice  with  lattice  vectors  A1  = La1
and A2 = La2  and lattice points R = n1A1 + n2A2  with n1, n2 ∈ Z.  The supercell area is

                                             V  = |A1 × A2| = L2|a1 × a2|                                                       (1)

   The corresponding reciprocal lattice vectors are deﬁned as

                                    B1 =  2πV   (A2 × ˆz)        B2 =  2πV   (ˆz × A1) ,                                           (2)

   and satisfy Ai · Bj  = 2π δij.  Allowed reciprocal vectors are therefore G = m1B1 + m2B2  with
m1, m2 ∈ Z.  It is convenient to introduce primitive reciprocal vectors bi  via Bi =  2πL  bi  such that
ai · bj = δij.  Then the norm of G can be written as

                                       |G| =  2πL  |m|g        |m|g =√        mT g m ,                                               (3)

where  the  metric  tensor  in  reciprocal  space  is  deﬁned  by  gij  =  bi · bj.   The  reciprocal-cell  area
satisﬁes
                                           √  det g = |b1 × b2| =        1
                                                                        |a1 × a2|  ,                                                    (4)

so that eq. (1) can equivalently be written as V  = L2/√det g.
