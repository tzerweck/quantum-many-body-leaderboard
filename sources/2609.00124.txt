         Green-function Zeros Encode Competing Mott and Charge-ordering Scales

                   While  correlated  insulators  are  devoid  of  low-energy  quasiparticle  poles,  their  Green  functions
                retain clean momentum structure through zeros.  However, precisely what zeros imply is not clear.
                By studying the extended Hubbard model both analytically and numerically,  we establish a new
                paradigm for strongly correlated matter:  the dispersion of Green function zeros is determined by
                both microscopic spin-spin correlations and defect kinematics.  In fact, we find that the dispersion
                changes discontinuously across the transition between the Mott and the checkerboard charge-density
                wave phases as is expected for a first-order transition.  This physics is robust to the inclusion of
                further neighbor hopping which simply fine tunes the spin-correlation or charge-defect kinematics.
                We conclude that it is thedispersionof the Green-function zeros that encode the physics of ordering
                resultant from the strong correlations.

Auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (AFQMC), density-matrix embedding theory (DMET), and twist-averaged

to the half-filled external references.  The magnetization panel remains restricted to half filling because the doped
