        Partial  suppression of  magnetism  in the  square  lattice  SU(3)  Hubbard model

                                           Samuel  Bird,∗      Sebastian  Huber,  and  Jannes  Nys

                              Institute  for  Theoretical  Physics,  ETH  Zurich,  8093  Z¨urich,  Switzerland

                   The SU(N) Hubbard model is a natural extension of the SU(2) model.  However, even the N  = 3
                case remains poorly understood.  We report a substantially new ground-state phase diagram of the
                square lattice SU(3) Fermi-Hubbard model.  Using a backﬂow ansatz, we identify strong signatures
                of  a  Mott  transition  and  a  subsequent  magnetic  transition,  and  the  suppression  of  a  previously
                predicted magnetic phase.  We study the hole-doped model, identifying a transition from magnetic
                to  paramagnetic  behavior  in  the  strong-coupling  regime.   Our  ﬁndings  oﬀer  a  qualitatively  new
                ground  state  picture.   More  broadly,  our  work  suggests  a  path  to  study  general  SU(N)  Hubbard
                models with arbitrary ﬁlling and geometry.

  We clarify some speciﬁc choices made in the ans¨atze.  We chose a form of Slater determinant assuming the Slater
matrix  to  be  block  diagonal  in  the  ﬂavor  degree  of  freedom,  with  nonidentical  blocks.  This  allows  for  spontaneous
breaking of the SU(3) symmetry.

One could further allow for the breaking of ﬂavor sector number conservation using a fully general Slater matrix, but

also increase the number of parameters from 3 ×  (48 ×  144) to 144 ×  (3 ×  144).
  Our Jastrow correlator is chosen to introduce charge and spin correlation holes into the wave function.  We impose
SU(3) symmetry by making intra-ﬂavor and inter-ﬂavor Jastrow factors spin independent.

It’s  important  to  note  that  the  distance  here, |ri −  rj|,  is  calculated  in  the  Manhattan  metric  for  a  square  lattice.
With periodic boundary conditions, this means that the maximal distance between any 2 points on a L ×  L super-cell
is L.  This means that the Jastrow correlator introduces an additional 2 ×  12 variational parameters in the case of a
L = 12.
  The  total  number  of  variational  parameters  used  in  the  Slater-Jastrow  ansatz  is  20’760,  at  least  in  the  undoped
case.  For  a  general  SU(N),  with  Nf  fermions,  ﬂavor  balance,  and  L = √                 Ns,  the  number  of  variational  parameters
is  N  ×  (Nf /N  ×  L2)  + 2 ×  L  =  Nf  ×  Ns + 2 ×  L,  and  is  independent  of  the  number  of  ﬂavors  N.   The  backﬂow
corrections  [S1,  S2]  introduce  only  two  additional  variational  parameters,  increasing  the  total  number  to  20’762,  or
Nf ×  Ns + 2 ×  L + 2 in the general case.
  We  optimized  our  Slater  determinant  state  using  Stochastic  Gradient  Descent  for  1500  update  steps,  checking
Energy convergence at the end.  It is during this ﬁrst initialization that we perform our pinning procedure as outlined
in the main paper.  After this, we turned on the Jastrow correlator and optimized this using minSR until convergence
[S3, S4], which we found to perform very well and converge more stably than bare Stochastic Reconﬁguration.  Finally,
we  turned  on  the  backﬂow  corrections,  once  again  optimizing  using  minSR  until  convergence.  All  ﬁnal  simulations
were performed using a learning rate between 10−  2 and 10−  3, a sample size of 212, and with a diagonal oﬀset of 10−  4
in the minSR to aid stability as is common practice.
