          Efficient tensor network simulation of multi-emitter non-Markovian systems

                   Irene Papaefstathiou,1, 2  Daniel Malz,3  J. Ignacio Cirac,1, 2  and Mari Carmen Ba˜nuls1, 2
                  1Max-Planck-Institut  f¨ur  Quantenoptik,  Hans-Kopfermann-Str.  1,  D-85748  Garching,  Germany
                     2Munich  Center  for  Quantum  Science  and  Technology  (MCQST),  80799  Munich,  Germany
                                    3Department  of  Mathematical  Sciences,  University  of  Copenhagen,
                                              Universitetsparken  5,  2100  Copenhagen,  Denmark
                                                              (Dated:  August 22, 2025)
                    We  present  a  numerical  method  to  simulate  a  system  of  multiple  emitters  coupled  to  a  non-
                 interacting bath, in any parameter regime.  Our method relies on a Block Lanczos transformation
                 that maps the whole system onto a strip geometry, whose width is given by the number of emitters.
                 Utilizing  the  spatial  symmetries  of  the  problem  and  identifying  the  relevant  range  of  energies  of
                 the bath we achieve a more efficient simulation, which we perform using tensor network techniques.
                 As a demonstration,  we study the collective emission from multiple emitters coupled to a square
                 lattice of bosons and observe how the departure from Markovianity as coupling strength and emitter
                 number is increased prevents collective radiation.  We also simulate the dynamic preparation of an
                 excitation in a bound state from a multi-excitation initial state.  Our work opens new possibilities
                 for the systematic exploration of non-Markovian effects in the dynamics and equilibrium properties
                 of multi-emitter systems.  Furthermore, it can easily be extended to other setups, including finite
                 bath temperature or impurities coupled to fermionic environments.
